Federal US Laws Against Stalking & Harassment

PUBLISHED / 07.22.2026
AUTHOR / RHIANNON WILLIAMS

The following federal statutes and provisions may be relevant to certain forms of stalking, harassment, unauthorized access, communications abuse, and related conduct. The applicability of any law depends on the facts and circumstances.

01 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 875(c)

Interstate Threats. Makes it a federal felony to transmit, in interstate or foreign commerce, any communication containing a true threat to injure another person. Applies to communications transmitted by telephone, email, text message, social media, or the Internet. Penalty: up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine.

02 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 1028 or 18 U.S.C. § 1028A

May apply if the perpetrator impersonates the victim or uses the victim's identifying information unlawfully while creating accounts or subscriptions.

03 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act)

Prohibits unauthorized access to protected computers and online accounts, including conduct involving hacking, unauthorized access to government systems, or using compromised accounts to facilitate stalking, harassment, or other crimes.

04 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. §§ 1030(a)(1)–(5)

May apply where unauthorized access is used to submit fraudulent online forms, place unwanted orders, create abusive subscriptions, interfere with computer systems, or obtain information without authorization.

05 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 1343

Wire Fraud. Prohibits schemes to defraud using interstate wire communications, including the Internet, email, or telephone.

06 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 1029(a)

Access Device Fraud. Criminalizes fraud involving unauthorized access devices, account numbers, or authentication credentials affecting interstate or foreign commerce.

07 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2261A

Interstate Stalking (Violence Against Women Act, as amended). Makes it a federal felony to use interstate travel, mail, telephones, or electronic communications (including the Internet) to engage in stalking or harassment that causes substantial emotional distress or places a person in reasonable fear of death or serious bodily injury.

08 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

47 U.S.C. § 223(a)(1)(C)

Harassing Telecommunications. Prohibits using telecommunications devices with intent to annoy, abuse, harass, or threaten another person under circumstances specified by the statute.

09 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

47 U.S.C. § 227

Regulates automated calls and text messages. While it primarily governs the entities sending robocalls or autodialed texts, it may become relevant if fraudulent enrollment causes unlawful automated communications to be sent to the victim.

10 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2425

Use of Interstate Facilities to Entice a Minor. Makes it a federal crime to knowingly use the Internet, telephone, or other means of interstate commerce to communicate with a minor with intent to entice or solicit unlawful sexual activity.

11 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) , including 18 U.S.C. §§ 2510–2523 , and the Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. §§ 2701–2713)

May apply where electronic communications are unlawfully intercepted or where stored electronic communications or accounts are accessed without authorization.

12 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2511

Prohibits the unlawful interception of wire, oral, or electronic communications, including certain unauthorized recordings of telephone calls or interception of emails while in transit.

13 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2701 and 18 U.S.C. § 1030

May apply to unauthorized access to another person's email account, cloud account, or other protected computer systems.

14 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. §§ 2511, 2512, and 1030

May apply to the installation or use of spyware, keyloggers, or other interception or credential-stealing software, depending on the circumstances.

15 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2512

Prohibits manufacturing, distributing, possessing, or advertising certain devices primarily designed for the surreptitious interception of electronic communications.

16 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2520

Provides a civil cause of action for persons whose communications have been unlawfully intercepted in violation of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act.

17 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2707

Provides a civil cause of action for violations of the Stored Communications Act involving unauthorized access to stored electronic communications.

18 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 3771

Crime Victims' Rights Act. Provides victims in federal criminal proceedings with rights including notice of proceedings, the opportunity to be heard, and treatment with fairness, dignity, and respect.

19 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

18 U.S.C. § 2265

Full Faith and Credit for Protection Orders. Requires states, tribes, and U.S. territories to recognize and enforce qualifying protection orders issued by other jurisdictions.

20 / FEDERAL REFERENCE

TAKE IT DOWN Act (Public Law 119-12)

Establishes federal criminal penalties and takedown procedures for the knowing publication or threatened publication of nonconsensual intimate visual depictions, including qualifying AI-generated intimate images, as provided by the Act.

Mindfulness Therapy Does Not Work For Everyone

PUBLISHED / 07.22.2026
AUTHOR / RHIANNON WILLIAMS

While mindfulness is often praised for reducing stress, a 2024 study found that during tough times, hope – not mindfulness – was the real key to resilience and professional focus. Read the source ↗

Mindfulness-based therapies have become a prominent part of modern mental health care through the convergence of Buddhist contemplative practices, medical research, and evidence-based psychology. As mindfulness was incorporated into approaches such as "DBT" and "trauma-informed care", it became increasingly common in clinical practice. As a result, some patients find it difficult to access alternative therapeutic approaches. While no single therapy works for everyone, treatments such as traditional Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) are well-established options, particularly for individuals seeking alternatives to mindfulness-based techniques. We recommend working with a licensed mental health professional while we conduct our investigation. Whether you are experiencing trauma, coordinated harassment, or both, a clinician can provide appropriate care while documenting the psychological impact when relevant.

Mindfulness can be effective for managing mild to moderate stress, anxiety, depression, and chronic pain, but it is not a cure-all. It is not a primary treatment for PTSD, psychotic disorders, OCD, or ongoing, reality-based threats such as digital stalking or harassment. For some individuals, mindfulness may increase distress, making alternatives such as traditional Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) or trauma-focused therapies a better fit.
